## 1. Overview — Hire Scout as "my assistant"

Scout Desktop is closer to a tireless chief of staff. Decisions are still made by a **person**, while Scout **prepares options, writes drafts, and keeps the calendar tidy** so important items are not missed. There are two main ways to work with Scout.

| Method | How | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 💬 **Ask in plain language (Just ask)** | Type what you need in natural language | "Schedule 30 minutes with the design team next week," "Book our usual room for Thursday," "Draft a polite decline for this invitation" |
| 📁 **Drag and drop (Drag & drop)** | Move email into a labeled Outlook folder | Scout handles replies, summaries, approvals, declines, and bookings on schedule — no typing required |

The point where these two methods come together into a single system is the **folder system** (Section 6).

The Scout EA package provides a feature set optimized for executive support.

## 4. How it works — Skills when needed, automations on schedule

Scout operates on two axes: **Skills** and **Automations**.

| Category | What | When it runs |
|---|---|---|
| 🧠 **Skills** | Playbooks loaded when a task matches — such as `schedule-meeting`, `inbox`, and `book-room` | When the user asks, or when Scout **automatically selects** the right skill |
| 🔁 **Automations** | Scheduled tasks that run on their own — 8 a.m. briefings, Friday focus-time planning, action folder processing every 30 minutes, and so on | On a **fixed schedule** |

<div class="info-box note" markdown="1">

**The bridge between the two is the folder system.** When users classify email into folders, automations wake up on schedule, open each folder, and run the matching skill. This is how "drag email here" turns into "Scout drafts the reply."
</div>

## 5. Action folder system — Turning Outlook into Scout's work queue

Create an assistant folder under your inbox (for example, `Inbox / 🗂️ Assistant`) and add the subfolders below. Drag any email into one of them, and Scout picks it up on the next run.

| Subfolder | What it does |
|---|---|
| ✉️ **Reply · Principal** | Drafts a reply **in the principal's (your) voice** |
| ✉️ **Reply · Assistant** | Drafts a reply **from the assistant (Scout)** |
| 📖 **Read** | **Summarizes** the folder contents into one clean digest |
| ✅ **Approve** | **Processes** approval items with risk gating |
| 📅 **Schedule** | Checks candidate times and **books** the meeting |
| 🟢 **Learn · Accept** | **Learns** automatic acceptance patterns |
| 🔴 **Learn · Decline** | **Learns** decline patterns (decline reasons stay generic and private) |
| 🗃️ **Done** | **Archives** email whose loop is closed |

**Draft-first example — Reply · Principal**

1. Drag an email that needs a reply from the principal (first person, principal's voice).
2. Scout creates a **thread-preserving draft** as if the principal wrote it directly — no assistant signature.
3. It tags the original with `Scout: Drafted` and leaves it in the folder **until it is sent**.
4. A human reviews and sends it in Outlook → Scout moves it to **Done**.

## 6. Drag-to-act loop

One simple habit runs the whole system.

1. **The user classifies** — Drag email into the folder that matches the desired task: reply, summarize, approve, or schedule.
2. **Scout runs** — On schedule (up to every 30 minutes), it opens each folder and runs the matching skill.
3. **Scout drafts or processes** — It creates drafts, books rooms, completes approvals, and tags status such as `Scout: Drafted`.
4. **The user reviews and sends** — Review the draft in Outlook and send it. When the loop closes, Scout moves the original to **Done**.

<div class="info-box note" markdown="1">

**Always draft-first.** Scout does **not send email directly** from folders on the user's behalf — it only prepares it, and a human clicks Send. Automatically completed approvals and bookings are also risk-gated; anything unusual stops and asks for confirmation.
</div>

## 7. Email & replies — Classify, draft, and clean up in the right voice

| Feature | Description |
|---|---|
| **Reply · Principal** | Drafts a reply **in the principal's voice** (first person, no assistant signature). Review before sending. |
| **Reply · Assistant** | Drafts a reply **from Scout itself (the AI assistant)** (third person about the principal, with an assistant signature). Suitable for coordination and logistics. |
| **Read** | Combines everything in the folder into one clean digest email, letting you skim without a pile of threads. |
| **Voice switching ("obo")** | Sets **who the message should sound like** — write as the principal or write as Scout. The two Reply folders select this automatically. |

In addition: priority tiers, automatic classification, deletion patterns, newsletter digests, thread-preserving drafts, and loop closure.

## 8. Meetings & rooms — From "finding a time" to "a scheduled meeting with a room"

- **📅 Schedule meetings** — Resolves attendees to real addresses, finds their assistants when available, checks availability, and proposes **3–5 courteous candidate times**.
- **🟢 Learn accept / 🔴 learn decline** — Drag invitations into `Learn · Accept` or `Learn · Decline` to learn the principal's patterns. It learns which invitations to process automatically, and keeps declines generic and private.
- **🏢 Book rooms** — Knows and books preferred rooms, including rooms that open only when an invitation is forwarded. Selects based on attendee count.
- **🔁 Scheduling processing** — Scans email and Teams for replies like "How about this time," checks the calendar, and converts them into invitations or tentative holds.

<div class="info-box note" markdown="1">

**Keep options open with holds.** When Scout proposes times, it creates **tentative calendar holds** linked to the original thread, then automatically cleans them up once the meeting is confirmed.
</div>

## 9. Approvals & expenses — Handling administrative work with guardrails

**Approve folder** — Drag approval email (expenses, POs, access, time off, credential renewals) into `Approve`. Once a day, Scout batch-organizes completed items and processes **up to five live approvals** with risk gating. Standard low-risk items are approved automatically, while **high-risk, ambiguous, or over-threshold** items **pause** and ask for confirmation.

**Expense reports** — Point Scout to receipts, and it creates a complete, review-ready expense report (travel, recurring monthly subscriptions, hotel itemization, corporate card matching). It **stops at a draft, and submission is always done by a human**.

**When Scout pauses**

| Condition |
|---|
| High-value item · Deny/Revoke · unusual permission access · departed employee · missing justification · anything ambiguous |

## 10. Automations — Ten scheduled runs

Start with read-only automations, then turn on the rest one by one as trust builds.

| Run time | Automation | Details |
|---|---|---|
| Weekdays 08:00 | **Daily meeting briefing** | Today's meetings, attendees, preparation flags + deliverable countdowns |
| Weekdays 07:30 | **1:1 acceptance monitor** | Shows 1:1s in the next five business days where the other person has not accepted yet |
| Sunday 08:00 | **Weekly preparation briefing** | Sunday reset: calendar risks, Monday launchpad, two-week lookahead |
| Weekdays 08:00 | **Product briefing digest** | Combines the briefing folder into one executive-ready summary email |
| Friday 09:15 | **Weekly focus-time planner** | Books next week's focus blocks without overbooking |
| Weekdays 12:00 | **Calendar changes + classification + hold cleanup** | Event classification, focus block conflict resolution, tentative hold cleanup |
| Daily 08:00 & 17:00 | **Personal calendar → work mirroring** | Mirrors personal events to the work calendar as private busy blocks (content hidden) |
| Weekdays every 30 minutes | **Action folder processing** | Reply/Read/Learn folder work: drafts, digests, learned invitation rules |
| Weekdays every 30 minutes | **Draft queue** | Maintains reply drafts and closes the loop when they are sent from Outlook |
| Weekdays 10:00 | **Approval folder (once per day)** | Batch-organizes completed approvals, then processes up to five live approvals with risk gating |

<div class="info-box note" markdown="1">

**Turn it on gradually.** First watch briefings and monitors (read-only), then calendar hygiene. Turn on draft and approval automations only after you trust the draft quality.

## 11. Installation — Let Scout handle it and answer a few questions

The easiest path is to **hand the package to Scout and answer the interview**. You do not need to edit anything by hand. Download the pack, open Scout Desktop, and paste the prompt below. Scout reads the built-in setup runbook, **interviews** you, and then performs the full installation: filling templates, creating Outlook folders, installing skills, and scheduling automations. It asks for confirmation **before each change**.

**Prompt to give Scout (example)**

```text
I want to set up my Scout-based assistant using the Scout EA package. Open the package's setup runbook
(SCOUT-SETUP.md, inside the Scout EA package folder I received) and follow it from beginning to end.
Interview me for the required information a little at a time. Then execute the steps in order:
fill in the templates, create my Outlook action folders, install the skills, and create the automations
(enable only the read-only ones first). For any task that makes a change, such as creating folders,
installing files, or sending messages, ask me for confirmation before running it. Do not guess names,
email addresses, or meeting rooms; ask me. Keep email draft-first (either in the principal's name or
Scout's name). When you are done, give me a summary and one first test.
```

**The interview works like this**

1. **Scout reads the runbook** — It opens `SCOUT-SETUP.md` and `CONFIG.md` to understand what to ask and what to do.
2. **Interview** — It asks about the principal, assistant identity, leadership and organization, priority contacts, projects, rooms, and logistics in a few small groups of questions. Unused items are skipped.
3. **Runbook execution** — It fills every `{% raw %}{{placeholder}}{% endraw %}`, creates Outlook folders, installs skills, and creates automations. It asks for user confirmation for each change.
4. **Start operations** — It turns on read-only briefings first, then provides a summary and the first test.

## 13. Trust & safety

Scout is designed to protect the principal's privacy and keep the user in control.

- **🖐️ Sending is approved by a human** — Folder-based email is draft-only. Nothing goes out until you review it and click Send.
- **🔒 Private by default** — Declines stay generic, and personal or travel information never leaks into messages that others see.
- **⏸️ Pauses on risk** — Approvals stop on high-value, destructive, or ambiguous items and ask for user judgment.
- **🧭 Stays quiet** — Automations notify you only when something actually needs attention — no "nothing to do" noise.
